## REMARKS
I. Introduction
Claims 1-16 stand rejected in the Non-Final Office Action mailed January 12, 2026. Applicant respectfully traverses the rejections and submits amended claims 1-16. The amendments clarify and narrow the claims to more precisely recite the inventive concept disclosed in the present application and discussed during the February 19, 2026 interview with the Examiner.
Applicant respectfully submits that amended independent claim 1 now recites a specific retrofit architecture and a specific snow-operational mapping and localization workflow that are not taught or suggested by the cited combinations. The dependent claims have also been revised so that they now depend from and are consistent with amended claim 1.
II. Record of Interview
An interview was conducted on February 19, 2026 between Applicant Rajeev Thakur and Examiner Alfred H. Tsui. As reflected in the Interview Summary of record, the issues discussed included proposed amendments, additional clarity regarding retrofit implementation, and the path forward for the claims. The Examiner indicated that the claims would require further search and consideration and that no agreement had been reached. Applicant was informed that the Examiner awaited Applicant's next action.
The substance of the interview included discussion of: (1) clarifying that the claimed machine is formed by converting a manually operated snowblower; (2) reciting a hybrid power architecture in which a gasoline engine remains dedicated to powering the auger while mobility and control functions are electrically powered; (3) reciting a snow-specific mapping workflow in which a map is created during snow-free or low-snow conditions when driveway, sidewalk, road, and related boundary edges are visible, and later used when snow obscures those boundaries; and (4) preserving manual operation through a disengageable clutch mechanism so that the machine can be manually positioned and handled when needed.
III. Support for the Amendments
The amendments are supported by the application as filed and published as US 2024/0209579 A1.
For example: paragraphs [0017]-[0024] describe converting an existing manual snowblower and using electric power for steering, movement, and apparatus control while retaining the original power source for the auger; paragraph [0021] explains that the invention solves the power problem by making steering, movement, and apparatus control electric while retaining gas-powered operation for the power-hungry snow-removal function; paragraph [0024] explains the division of the machine into a gas-powered auger function, electric movement/steering/attachment control, and electric sensing/perception/control; paragraph [0031] describes rear wheels powered directly by DC motors, a manually disengageable clutch mechanism, electrically powered auger engagement, and electrically powered chute and deflector control; paragraph [0032] describes the edge computer module, camera sensors, GPS, IMU, wheel speed sensor, optional static sensors, optional signposts, and front swivel wheels; and paragraphs [0047]-[0051] describe manual clutch disengagement for positioning, autonomous operation, and creating a map when there is little or no snow so that the driveway and sidewalk boundaries are visible for later use when snow obscures those boundaries.
Applicant respectfully submits that the amendments do not introduce new matter, but instead clarify and more precisely claim the invention already disclosed.
IV. Overview of the Amended Independent Claim
Amended claim 1 is no longer directed broadly to any self-operating autonomous snow-removal machine using sensors and an edge computer. Instead, amended claim 1 now recites a specific machine formed by converting a manually operated snowblower and having a particular architecture and workflow, including:
(a) a manually operated snowblower having a gasoline engine that drives the auger and rear wheels that originally are free-spinning and pushed by a user; (b) a drive conversion assembly with at least one electric motor mechanically coupled to at least one rear wheel through a manually engageable and disengageable clutch mechanism; (c) electrically powered actuation for engaging and disengaging the belt drive between the gasoline engine and the auger and for rotating the chute, and optionally moving the deflector; (d) an onboard sensor suite and edge computer module; (e) a training mode performed when snow is absent or low enough that driveway, sidewalk, road, and related boundary edges are visible to create and store a map; and (f) an autonomous snow-removal mode performed later when snow partially or fully obscures those boundary edges, with operation based on the stored map and current onboard sensor data.
Amended claim 1 further recites that during autonomous snow removal the gasoline engine provides power for rotating the auger and does not provide motive power to the rear wheels, and that the machine remains manually operable via the disengageable clutch. The claim also clarifies that localization is performed primarily using the onboard sensor suite and the stored map, with any static sensors or signposts on the property, if present, providing only supplemental safety and localization information.
These limitations are material and substantially narrow the claim relative to the original version.
V. Traversal of Rejection of Claims 1-2 and 4-6 over Ramanand in view of Eavenson
The Office Action states that claims 1-2 and 4-6 are rejected over Ramanand in view of Eavenson. Applicant respectfully traverses at least with respect to amended claim 1.
The Office Action relies on Ramanand for a retrofit kit, electric motors, edge-computer control, a stored map, and manual mode, and relies on Eavenson for a hybrid snow thrower. The Office Action then concludes that it would have been obvious to apply Ramanand's retrofit kit to Eavenson's manual powered engine vehicle.
Applicant respectfully submits that amended claim 1 is patentably distinct from this combination for at least the following reasons.
First, amended claim 1 requires a specific power split in the context of a converted manually operated snowblower: the gasoline engine provides power for rotating the auger and does not provide motive power to the rear wheels during autonomous snow removal, while the rear wheels are driven by at least one electric motor. This is not merely an abstract retrofit of autonomous controls to a powered vehicle. It is a specific functional division between a high-power snow-clearing subsystem and a separately electrified motive/control subsystem. The present application repeatedly identifies this division as the core technical solution. See at least paragraphs [0021], [0022], [0024], [0037], and [0040].
Second, amended claim 1 requires a drive conversion assembly in which the at least one electric motor is mechanically coupled to at least one rear wheel through a clutch mechanism that is manually engageable and disengageable to selectively allow free-rolling manual movement of the machine. This limitation is directed to preserving the practical usability of a converted manual snowblower, not merely providing a generic 'manual mode.' The specification explains that the clutch is disengaged so the machine can be rolled to the start position and later handled like the original manual machine. See paragraphs [0031] and [0047].
Third, amended claim 1 requires a snow-specific mapping workflow in which the map is created during no-snow or low-snow conditions when driveway, sidewalk, road, and other boundary edges are visible, and then used later when snow partially or fully obscures those boundaries. This is a significant narrowing limitation directed to the specific technical problem of snow removal, where visual boundaries present during setup disappear during operation. The Office Action cites Ramanand paragraph 0128 for a global map, but amended claim 1 requires more than a generic stored map. It requires the specific timing and purpose of the map creation and later use under snow-obscured conditions. The present application describes this specifically at paragraph [0050].
Fourth, amended claim 1 requires that localization during autonomous snow removal is performed primarily using the onboard sensor suite and the stored map, with any static sensors or signposts on the property, if present, providing only supplemental safety and localization information. This clarifies that external infrastructure is not the primary localization mechanism. The present application expressly presents static sensors and signposts as optional supplemental aids. See paragraphs [0032], [0042], [0043], and [0051].
Accordingly, even assuming Ramanand teaches a retrofit kit and Eavenson teaches a snow thrower, the cited combination does not teach or suggest the amended claim as a whole, including the specific converted snowblower architecture, the specific division of engine power versus electric motive power, the manually disengageable wheel-drive clutch preserving free-rolling manual movement, and the specific snow-obscured map-training/localization workflow.
For these reasons, Applicant respectfully submits that amended claim 1, and claims depending therefrom, are patentably distinct over Ramanand in view of Eavenson.
VI. Traversal of Rejections Based on Ramanand, Eavenson, and Lee
The Office Action rejects claims 7, 10, 13-15 over Ramanand and Eavenson in view of Lee. Applicant has amended these claims to better align with amended claim 1 and the disclosed invention.
As amended, claim 13 now recites a wireless communication interface configured to communicate with a smartphone application, wherein the smartphone application is configured to permit manual control or boundary-teaching input. Applicant acknowledges that smartphone interaction may be taught broadly in the art. However, amended claim 13 now depends from substantially narrowed claim 1 and therefore inherits the limitations discussed above.
As amended, claims 14 and 15 clarify that static sensors and signposts, if present, are supplemental aids. This amendment is directly responsive to the Office Action's reliance on Lee for static sensor communication and static signs. The invention as claimed is not centered on static external infrastructure as a primary localization mechanism. Rather, any such infrastructure is optional and supplemental. That distinction is supported by paragraphs [0032], [0042], [0043], and [0051].
Applicant respectfully submits that the amended dependent claims are at least patentable by virtue of depending from amended claim 1 and further narrowing it.
VII. Traversal of Rejections Based on Ramanand, Eavenson, and Frick
The Office Action rejects claims 8-9, 11, and 16 over Ramanand and Eavenson in view of Frick. Applicant has amended these claims to make clear that they are directed to possible extensions or applications of the disclosed architecture, while still depending from amended claim 1.
Claim 11 has also been amended to more concretely recite that the one or more snow-clearing zones include driveway, sidewalk, road, parking area, or other paved area. This language ties more directly to the disclosed implementation and avoids unnecessary abstraction.
Again, even if Frick discloses autonomous utility vehicles in other contexts, those teachings do not cure the deficiencies of the primary combination as applied to amended claim 1.
VIII. Traversal of Rejections Based on Ramanand, Eavenson, and Pitcel
The Office Action rejects claims 3 and 12 over Ramanand and Eavenson in view of Pitcel.
Applicant has amended claim 3 to specifically recite front swivel wheels attached to a front portion of the machine to facilitate turning of the machine, consistent with paragraph [0032] and paragraph [0044] of the present application.
Applicant has amended claim 12 to recite warning outputs for pedestrian safety. Again, even if Pitcel discloses such features broadly, those teachings do not render amended claim 1 obvious for the reasons discussed above.
IX. Remarks Regarding the Original Claim Scope
Applicant respectfully acknowledges that the originally filed claim 1 was broad and could be read to encompass generic combinations of retrofit autonomy, a stored map, and manual mode. The present amendments are intended to directly address the prior art cited by the Examiner by focusing the claim on the actual inventive concept described throughout the specification and discussed during the interview.
In particular, the amended claims now focus on: (1) conversion of a manually operated snowblower; (2) gasoline power dedicated to auger rotation; (3) electric motive and control subsystems for the rear wheels and attachments; (4) preservation of manual free-rolling movement through a disengageable clutch; and (5) a map created during visible-boundary conditions and later used when snow obscures those boundaries, with any static sensors or signposts being merely supplemental if present.
Applicant respectfully submits that these amendments materially distinguish the claimed invention from the cited combinations.
