Rejections under 35 USC 103
A prima facie case of obviousness is established when an examiner provides:
1. one or more references
2. that were available to the inventor and
3. that teach
4. a suggestion to combine or modify the references,
5. the combination or modification of which would appear to be sufficient to have made
the claimed invention obvious to one of ordinary skill in the art.
Accordingly, an applicant who is able to prove that the Examiner has failed to establish
any one of these elements will prevent the prima facie case of obviousness from being
established.
Under Graham v. John Deere Co. the standard of obvious used by the Examiner to
support the obviousness rejection is incorrect. The Examiner must view the claim as a whole
and not merely the differences between the present invention and the prior art.
Here, Examiner has done nothing more with respect to the other than to list the claim
elements/limitations, and merely citing a teaching of the individual element in the prior art.
Thus, it is not correct for an Examiner to focus on the differences between the prior art
and the claimed invention and then to state that the differences themselves or individually are obvious, there must be some expectation that the combination of the two would be successful
and there must be motivation to combine.
Here, there is no expectation that merely combining the two systems would be easily and readily apparent to one of ordinary skill in the art. If such a combination is obvious and
easily enabled, Examiner should quickly and easily be able to provide a citation for just such a
combination.

With respect to claims 4 and 8, Section 2144.03 of the Manual of Patent Examining
Procedure (MPEP) provides guidelines for relying on Official Notice and personal knowledge.
Official Notice is permissible only in limited circumstances. Official Notice is permissible only for certain types of facts; those that are capable of instant and unquestionable demonstration as
being well-known. It is never appropriate to rely solely on Official Notice as the principal
evidence upon which a rejection was based. Instead, Official Notice is only appropriate for facts
that are of notorious character and that serve to fill in the gaps that might exist in the evidentiary
showing to support a particular ground of rejection. (MPEP § 2144.03(A)).

error(s) in the Examiner's action, which would include stating why the noticed fact is not

room.

and their desired size range for optimal performance. The prior art does not use, teach, or
suggest the novel and non-obvious combination taught by the present invention which is why the
Examiner can not readily find prior art teaching these limitations and is taking unsupported Official Notice in an effort to improperly reject the claims.
With respect to claim 8, none of the cited prior art suggests a battery back as both Lin
and Maker required much more sophisticated electronics to run their devices and could not be
enabled easily by a battery pack. It would not have been obvious to a POSTIA as alleged by the Examiner in their reasoning for a POSITA to use a battery pack for the devices taught by Lin or Maker since both require additional electronics and power to run, where the use of a battery pack
to power them would make them non-functional for their intended purpose.
