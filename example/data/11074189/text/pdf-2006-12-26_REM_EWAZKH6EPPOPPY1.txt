The present response is directed to the Office Action mailed September
18, 2006 as well as the phone interview conducted October 11, 2006 and the
subsequent Interview Summary mailed October 13, 2006. By way of the present
response, claims 1-9, 11-16 and 18-22 remain pending. In support of the pending
claims, Applicant further submits an affidavit according to 37 C.F.R. §1.132 as is

Phone Interview

Applicant respectfully thanks the Examiner for the courtesy shown Applicant’s
representative during a phone interview conducted on October 11, 2006. Applicant
herein confirms the substance of the interview as expressed in the October 13, 2006
Interview Summary. In response to said phone interview, Applicant herein presents

Claim Rejections — 35 U.S.C. § 103
In the Office Action mailed September 18, 2006, claims 1-9, 11-16 and 18-22

were rejected under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) as being unpatentable over Rasmussen in view
of Dartey et al. and Lengerich et al. In rejecting independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22,
the Examiner noted that the claimed sugar particle size would be obvious to one of
skill in the art and further noted in the October 13, 2006 Interview Summary that said
particle sizes were deemed an obvious variation to a known component absent a
showing of unexpected results or criticality.

As discussed within the attached affidavit of Susan M. Hayes-Jacobsen, the
presently claimed cookie units and related method of making must not only satisfy
traditional cookie characteristics including processing, storage and taste but also must
maintain a visible image when a cookie intermediate is baked. As discussed in the
affidavit, initial product runs using the conventional sugar grind at the time resulted
in unsatisfactory products. In response, a variety of production tests including
different sugar grinds were conducted wherein the presently claimed sugar particle
size limitations were surprisingly discovered. While this balance may not be crucial to
other cookie dough applications, visual appearance and consistency are absolute
necessities for the presently claimed cookie units and as such, the claimed sugar grind

is indeed a critical element of the presently claimed invention.

While Applicant’s acknowledge the assertion that a variety of sugar grinds are
commercially available and that sugar particle size can be manipulated, Applicant’s
respectfully suggest that the Examiner has trivialized what the Applicant’s have found
to be critical elements necessary for success in the presently claimed product, cookie
units having multiple portions in which a visible element must be recognizable upon
baking. Applicant’s who have years in the present field certainly do not consider the
necessity of using the claimed sugar grind as obvious. In fact, Applicant’s were forced
to conduct numerous experiments and field trials to recognize a problem existed due
to the unique nature of the product, let alone discover a sugar grind that produceda
satisfactory product. For these reasons, Applicant’s respectfully request that if the
Examiner maintains the conclusion that the presently claimed sugar grind is obvious,
that the Examiner acknowledge that Official Notice is being taken and that

documentary evidence in support of these findings be provided.

With respect to the specific 35 U.S.C. 103(a) rejections, the prior Office Action
mailed September 18, 2006, acknowledges that Rasmussen fails to disclose first and
second dough compositions with the claimed sugar. In addition, the disclosures of
Dartey et al. and Lengerich et al. are similarly devoid of any mention of the claimed
sugar particle sizes. As discussed in the affidavit and above, the claimed sugar
particle sizes are indeed a critical element for product success, a cookie in which an
extruded visible image must remain recognizable following baking. For at least these
reasons, the Applicant requests withdrawal of the present rejections as a prima facie
case of obviousness has not been established.

Applicants respectfully request reconsideration and allowance of the pending
claims as it is Applicants’ belief that the rejections have been overcome. The Examiner
is encouraged to contact the undersigned if it will assist in advancing prosecution of
the pending claims.

Please apply any charges or refunds to Deposit Account No. 07-0900 and

provide notification of such transaction(s) to the address below.
