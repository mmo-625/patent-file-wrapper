Claim 22 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. 112, second paragraph, as being indefinite
for failing to particularly point out and distinctly claim the subject matter which applicant
regards as the invention.

In claim 22, the phrase “ said dough intermediate” does not have antecedent basis.

The following is a quotation of 35 U.S.C. 103(a) which forms the basis for all

obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:

(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set
forth in section 102 of this title, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and
the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the
invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains.

_ Patentability shall not be negatived by the manner in which the invention was made.

The factual inquiries set forth in Graham v. John Deere Co., 383 U.S. 1, 148
USPQ 459 (1966), that are applied for establishing a background for determining
obviousness under 35 U.S.C. 103(a) are summarized as follows:

Determining the scope and contents of the prior art.

Ascertaining the differences between the prior art and the claims at issue.
Resolving the level of ordinary skill in the pertinent art.

Considering objective evidence present in the application indicating
obviousness or nonobviousness.

Claims 1-22 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over
Rasmussen in view of Dartey et al.

Rasmussen discloses a cookie dough intermediate having and integral design
element. The dough intermediate is made from two dough mixes having different
identifiable indicia such as different color characteristics or texture characteristics. The |
inner and outer cookie doughs form and interior cross sectional shape of dough that is |
visible. The dough intermediate is formed by simultaneously extruding two different

doughs through a pair of concentric dies. The co-extruded dough has an internal or

- Rasmussen does not disclose a dough containing shortening with a SFI between

Dartey et al disclose a cookie dough. They disclose that shortening having a

It would have been obvious to one skilled in the art to use shortening having the
claimed solid fat index because Dartey et al disclose such shortening is normally used
in cookie dough. It would have been obvious to combine flour, sugar, water and flour to .
make the cookie dough because such ingredients are common ingredients for cookie
dough as shown by Dartey et al. The Rasmussen dough has a particular configuration
and is made up of two different doughs. Since the dough intermediate is made by the
same method of coextruding the two doughs, it is obvious the dough can be made to
have integral design element that is visible on both sides and it would have been
obvious to one skilled in the art to do so when desiring to enhance the appearance of

the product. It would have been obvious to vary the dough proportion depending on the

configuration made, the particular design wanted and the taste, flavor and texture
desired. It would also have been obvious to one skilled in the art to change the shape
of the dies to make different external configurations and internal designs and to use
