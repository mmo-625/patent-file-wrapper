REMARKS

The present response is directed to the Office Action mailed March 16, 2007.

By way of the present response, claims 1-9, 11-16 and 18-22 remain pending.
Claim Rejections — 35 U.S.C. § 103

In the Office Action mailed March 16, 2007, claims 1-9, 11-16 and 18-22 were
rejected under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) as being unpatentable over Rasmussen in view of
Dartey et al., Lengerich et al., the book “PROFESSIONAL BAKING” and Gage et al. In
rejecting independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22, the Examiner has relied on the
teachings of “PROFESSIONAL BAKING” and Gage et al. as making it obvious to one
skilled in the art to size sugar so as to obtain optimum properties in cookies made.
Once again, Applicants respectfully traverse said rejection.

As has been discussed in prior responses, Applicants recognize that a variety of
sugar grinds are commercially available and that these sugar particle sizes can be
manipulated. However, neither PROFESSIONAL BAKING nor Gage et al. considered
individually or combination teach or suggest the claimed sugar grind, which
Applicants discussed in both the application as filed as well as the Affidavit of
December 18, 2006 as being surprising beneficial to product performance.

Within the Office Action mailed March 16, 2007, the suggestion is made that
the particle size range of the pending claims is conventional as taught by Gage et al.
Applicants respectfully disagree and instead directs the attention of the Examiner to
the numerous places in both the application and the previously submitted affidavit
wherein the characteristics of the presently claimed sugar are described (Application:
Page 10, Line 18 - Page 11, Line10; Affidavit: Paragraphs 7-10). Suitable sugar for use
in the present invention comprises only about 18% to about 45% of particles large
enough to be retained on a 100 mesh screen. Not one of the grinds disclosed in Gage
et al. satisfies this requirement. The most preferred grind referred to as Baker’s
Special comprises at least 59.1% of particles retained on a 100 mesh screen (Trace on
30 mesh + 0.4 on 40 mesh + 1.7 on 50 mesh + 24.8 on 80 mesh + 32.2 on 100 mesh).
In fact, the other preferred sugar taught by Gage et al. comprises at least 94.7% of

particles be retained on an even larger 80 mesh screen. Every one of these

“conventional” grinds shown in Table A of Gage et al. satisfies the definition of being
too course or too fine for use in the present application. One of skill in the art reading
the disclosure of Gage et al. would not be at all directed to arrive at the presently
claimed dough and instead, would have been directed to utilize sugar grinds that
Applicants have previously described in both the specification and affidavit as being
unsuitable for the present invention.

With reference to the Practical Baking disclosure, most of the variety of
granulated sugars discussed in Practical Baking can be found within Table A of Gage
et al., which satisfy the definition of being too fine or too course for use with the
present invention. Practical Baking is utterly devoid of any discussion relating to
custom grinding of sugar to achieve desirable results for cookie dough that must be
extrudable and have suitable expansion properties such that a continuous design
element be recognizable when a dough unit comprising at least two doughs is baked.

Not only do Gage et al. and Practical Baking fail to disclose the claimed sugar
particle size distribution, nowhere within any of their disclosures are there
suggestions that would lead one of skill in the art to the presently claimed
composition. Neither reference addresses the processing need for a dough that is
capable of being extruded so as to form a continuous design element nor is there any
discussion of baked expansion properties that allow the continuous design element to
remain recognizable when baked, let alone when baked from a cookie unit comprising
at least two doughs defining the continuous design element. Instead what is taught
by Gage et al. and Practical Baking is that a variety of sugar grinds are commercially
available having a wide variety of particle distribution ranges, none of which satisfy
the unique needs of the claimed invention.

For at least these reasons, Applicants request reconsideration and withdrawal
of the present rejections and allowance of the pending claims as it is Applicants belief
that the rejections have been overcome. The Examiner is encouraged to contact the

undersigned if it will assist in advancing prosecution of the pending claims.
