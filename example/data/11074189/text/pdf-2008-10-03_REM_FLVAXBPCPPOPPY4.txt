Claim Rejections — 35 U.S.C. § 103

Claims 1-9, 11-16, and 18-22 stand rejected under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) as being
unpatentable over U.S. Patent No. 5,620,713 to Rasmussen in view of U.S. Patent No. 4,894,246
to Dartey et al., U.S. Patent No. 5,079,012 to Lengerich et al., the book “Professional Baking,”
and U.S. Patent No. 4,892,745 to Gage et al. Applicants respectfully traverse the rejections.

As set forth at MPEP § 2144.05(II]), “[a]pplicants can rebut a prima facie case of
obviousness based on overlapping ranges by showing the criticality of the claimed range...‘In
such a situation, the applicant must show that the particular range is critical, generally by
showing that the claimed range achieves unexpected results relative to the prior art range.’”
(quoting In re Woodruff, 919 F.2d 1575 (Fed. Cir. 1990)).

Regarding the limitation of a shortening with a solid fat index (SFI) between about 22 to
about 35 at about 70°F recited in each independent claim 1, 14, 20-22, the Examiner states on
page 2 of the Office Action that Dartey et al. discloses “that shortening having a solid fat index
of about 15-25% at 70 degree[s] F is commonly used in cookie dough” indicating a slight
overlap at the bottom of the claimed SFI range and the top of the SFI range disclosed in Dartey
et al. Applicants submit that one of ordinary skill in the art would not look to the teachings of
Dartey et al. to provide the claimed SFI range. The criticality of the claimed SFI range is clearly

stated in the present application as originally filed at page 12, line 31- page 13, line 4: “If

Further, a “prima facie case of obviousness may also be rebutted by showing that the art,
in any material respect, teaches away from the claimed invention.” MPEP § 2144.05(II]) (citing
In re Geisler, 116 F.3d 1465, 1471 (Fed. Cir. 1997)). Regarding the limitation of water in an
amount from 4% to 15% by weight recited in each independent claim 1, 14, 20-22, the Examiner
states on page 3 of the Office Action that Lengerich et al. discloses “cookie dough to form
cookies using an extruder; the dough has a water content of from about .5 — 10% (see col. 11,
lines 65-66).” However, Lengerich et al. teaches away from the present, disclosing that the
“total moisture content of the cookie mixture is generally less than 10% and preferably less than
8%.” Abstract. Therefore, it would not be obvious to one or ordinary skill in the art to look to
the teachings of Lengerich et al. for the claimed water range.

1, 14, and 20-22, the Examiner states on page 3 of the Office Action that Gage et al. discloses

“[s]ugar wherein at least about 30% by weight remains on a US Screen mesh of 100 is most
