Dnexpected Results -

The present application is directed to cookie units formed by simultaneously
coextruding a first and second cookie dough having first and second colors $0 as to
define a continuous design element between a top surface and bottom surface of the
cookie unit. More specifically, the application and the pending claims address the
previously unidentified issue of maintaining the appearance and recognizability of the
design element during baking of the cookie unit, By formulating the first and second
cookie dough to have similar baked expansion properties through the claimed
manipulation of water content, sugar particle sizes and shortening selection, the
applicants achieved the unexpected result of not only having first and second cookie
dough with handling and processing properties suited to extrusion but also first and
second cookie dough that when baked, allows the continuous design element to
remain visually identifiable even when expanded through baking. None of the
presently cited prior art recognize the issue of maintaining the continuous design
element through the extrusion/baking process nor do they disclose the unexpected
advantages provided by the presently claimed dough formulations.

In the Final Office Action mailed April 11, 2006, claims 1-22 were rejected
under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) as being unpatentable over Rasmussen in view of Dartey et
al., Drantch et al., Hay, Jr. et al. and Lengerich et al.

Applicants have amended independent claims 2, 14 and 20-22 to further clarify
the presently claimed invention as Comprising first and second dough formulated to
have similar baked expansion properties.

As acknowledged by the Examiner in the Final Office Action mailed April 11,
2006, U.S. Patent No. 5,620,713 to Rasmussen does not disclose first and second
dough compositions with the claimed shortening, sugar and water. In addition,
Rasmussen lacks any disclosure relative to the desirability of the first and second
dough having similar baked expansion properties as present claimed. As described in
the present application, use of the claimed shortening and sugar is advantageous in
that the use of first and second dough with similar baked expansion properties allows
for the claimed continuous design element to retain its appearance and identifiable
characteristics through both the cutting and baling steps.

to dough and dough products and may individually disclose certain elements of the
pending claims, there is no suggestion or teaching within Rasmussen that using first
and second dough with similar baked expansion characteristics is advantageous in
retaining a continuous design element during the baking process. “The mere fact that
references can be combined or modified does not render the resultant combination’
