Claims 1-9, 11-16, 18-22 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being
unpatentable over Rasmussen in view of Dartey et al, Lengerich et al, the book “
Professional Baking” and Gage et al.

Rasmussen discloses a cookie dough intermediate having and integral design
element. The dough intermediate is made from two dough mixes having different
identifiable indicia such as different color characteristics or texture characteristics. The
inner and outer cookie doughs form and interior cross sectional shape of dough that is
visible. The dough intermediate is formed by simultaneously extruding two different
doughs through a pair of concentric dies. The co-extruded dough has an internal or
center portion dough that is of desired cross sectional shape and encased by a second
dough. The dough log is sliced to form individual units. The shape of an animal is
shown in figure 3 and 6. (see col. 1 lines 55-60 and col. 4 lines 47-55)

Rasmussen does not disclose a dough containing shortening with a SFI between
about 22-35 at about 70 degree F, dough having similar baked expansion properties the
water content of the dough, the ratio of the height and cross-sectional length, integral
design that is visible on both sides, the amount of first cookie dough, a third portion of
dough, design that is a particular theme such as seasonal, sporting and holiday, sugar
having the particle size distribution as claimed, adding inclusion, the type of cutter as
claimed, packaging the dough pieces and non-circular external shape.

Dartey et al disclose a cookie dough. They disclose that shortening having a
solid fat index of about 15-25% at 70 degree F is commonly used in cookie dough.

Dartey et al also disclose that the spread of the cookie dough can be reduced by

Gage et al disclose a process for reducing the time required to achieve texture
equilibration in cookies. They teach using sugar having particular particle size for
accelerating texture equilibration. Sugar wherein at least about 30% by weight remains
on a US Screen mesh of 100 is most preferred for use. ( see col. 7 line 49 through col.

The book teaches different types of sugar that are used in baked product. It
teaches very fine and ultra-fine sugars are prized for making cakes and cookies
because they make a more uniform batter and can support higher quantities of fat. It
also teaches that finer granulations are better for mixing into doughs and batters
because they dissolve more quickly. It also teaches that the particle size of the sugar
affect the spread of the cookies. Coarse granulated sugar increases spread, while fine
sugar or confectioners’ sugar reduces spread.

It would have been obvious to one skilled in the art to use shortening having the
claimed solid fat index because Dartey et al disclose such shortening is normally used
in cookie dough. It would have been obvious to combine flour, sugar, water and flour to

make the cookie dough because such ingredients are common ingredients for cookie

dough as shown by Dartey et al. As to the sugar particles, there are many types of
sugar available; for example, there are coarse sugar, fine sugar, very fine sugar etc..
The sugar can also be sized individually by grinding to fit a particular need. The prior
art to Gage et al and the cookbook teach that the particle size of the sugar can affect
the properties of the cookies. Thus, it would have been obvious to one skilled in the art
to size the sugar to obtain the most optimum properties in the cookies made. The
appropriate size of sugar is a result-effect variable which can be determined by one
skilled in the art to produce the most optimum product. Optimization is within the skill of

would have been obvious to add inclusions such as nut, fruit etc.. to enhance the flavor,

depending on the type of ingredients used and the texture wanted in the cookies. For
example, if syrup or liquid egg or milk, or honey etc.. are used in the dough formula,
then it would have been obvious to use less water than in formula without these liquid
ingredient. The water content is also affected by the texture desired. A dough
producing cookies with soft texture will have more water than dough producing cookies
with crispy texture. Since the making of cookies is notoriously well known in the art, it
would have been within the skill of one in the art to determine the appropriate amount of
water for the type of cookie made. The water content claimed is a conventional amount
as shown by Lengerich et al. It would have been obvious to form cookies form doughs
with similar expansion if ones wants the cookies to have similar configuration. It would

have been readily apparent to one skilled in the art because if one dough portion

In the response filed 7/16/07, applicant states the that the office action made the
suggestion that the particle size range of the pending claims is conventional as taught
by Gage et al and applicant disagrees with this position. The position taken in the office
action is that the particle sizes of the sugar affects the properties of the cookies. Thus,
it would have been obvious to one skilled in the art to size the sugar to obtain the most
optimum properties in the cookies made. The appropriate size of sugar is a result-effect
variable which can be determined by one skilled in the art to produce the most optimum
product. Optimization is within the skill of one in the art. In addition to this, the particle
size claimed is also conventional as shown in Gage. Applicant’s attention is directed to
col. 8 lines 42-45 where Gage et al disclose “ sugar having the particle size of Baker's
Special wherein at least about 30% by weight remains on a US Mesh size of 100 is
preferred for use”. Thirty percent is within the claimed range of 18-45%. Thus, in view
of both aspects, the claimed sugar size would have been an obvious parameter
because the prior art teaches different particle sizes for sugar and the different sizes

have an effect on the final outcome of the product. This demonstrates that the size is a
