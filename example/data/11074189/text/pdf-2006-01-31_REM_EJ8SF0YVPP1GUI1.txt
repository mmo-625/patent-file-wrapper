Rejections under 35 U.S.C. § 103

In the Office Action mailed September 29, 2005, claims 1-22 were rejected under 35
U.S.C. § 103(a) as being unpatentable over United States Patent No. 5,620,713 to Rasmussen
in view of U.S. Patent No. 4,894,246 to Dartey et al. In response, Applicants have amended
independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22 to further clarify the presently claimed invention.

Prima facie obviousness is not established if all the elements of the rejected claim are
not disclosed or suggested in the cited art. In re Ochiai, 37 USPQ 1127, 1131 (Fed. Cir. 1995).
("The test for obviousness vel non is statutory. It requires that one compare the claims’ ‘subject
matter as a whole’ with the prior art 'to which said subject matter pertains.'"). See also, MPEP
2143.03 "All Claim Limitations Must Be Taught or Suggested," citing In re Royka, 180 USPQ
580 (CCPA 1974). "To establish prima facie obviousness of a claimed invention, all of the claim
limitations must be taught or suggested by the prior art.". MPEP 2143.03.

A criteria to establish a prima facie case of obviousness is that there must be some
suggestion or motivation either in the references themselves or in the knowledge generally
available to one of ordinary skill in the art to combine the reference teachings. MPEP 2142-
2143. In re Vaeck, 947 F.2d 488, 20 USPQ 2d 1438 (Fed. Cir. 1991). Furthermore, the fact

that references can be combined does not render the combination obvious unless the prior art
suggests the desirability of the combination. MPEP2143.01, citing In re Mills, 16 U.S.P.Q.2d
1430, (Fed. Cir. 1990). As summarized by MPEP 2141.02 in a bold heading - "PRIOR ART
MUST BE CONSIDERED IN ITS ENTIRITY, INCLUDING DISCLOSURES THAT TEACH AWAY
FROM THE CLAIMS."

As presently amended, independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22 relate to dough units or
intermediates formed from a first and second dough wherein each dough has a moisture
content from about 4% to about 15% by weight to promote simultaneous extrusion of the first
and second dough. As discussed in the application as originally filed, a suitable extrudable
cookie dough for use with the presently claimed invention must be formulated so as be
extrudable and not too deformable at typical production rates (page 4, lines 21-23, page 12,
lines 23-27). Dartey et al. discloses the use of a casing dough and a filler dough to form a
multi-textured cookie, wherein the initial moisture content of the filler dough is generally at
least about 15% by weight more than the initial moisture content of the casing cookie dough.
(Col. 10, Lines 4-7). As such, at least one of the casing dough and filler dough taught by
Dartey et al. would fall outside the presently claimed moisture content and thus does teach or
suggest the presently claimed moisture contents for the first and second cookie dough.

In addition, the casing and filling dough disclosed by Dartey et al. would be unsuitable
for use in the extrusion process taught by Rasmussen as the increased moisture content of the
filler dough could not used successfully with the extrusion methods taught by Rasmussen. As
such, there is no suggestion or motivation to combine Rasmussen and Dartey et al. and
instead, the references teach away from such a combination and the modification suggested by
the Examiner can only be based on hindsight using Applicants’ specification as a template to
guide the modification. Such hindsight reasoning based on Applicants’ own specification is not
appropriate. See MPEP 2141.01(II]).

As neither Rasmussen nor Dartey el al. disclose, teach or suggest a first and second
dough having the presently claimed moisture contents, neither Rasmussen nor Dartey et al.,
considered individually or in combination, can render independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22 as
prima facie obvious. Thus, Applicants respectfully request withdrawal of the rejections to
independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22. While Applicants do not acquiesce with respect to specific
comments directed against dependent claims 2-13 and 15-19, Applicants do not comment

further on these issues presently since they are moot in view of the above comments.

Applicants respectfully request reconsideration and allowance of the pending claims as

kindly requested. The Examiner is encouraged to contact the undersigned if it will assist in

expediting prosecution of these claims.
