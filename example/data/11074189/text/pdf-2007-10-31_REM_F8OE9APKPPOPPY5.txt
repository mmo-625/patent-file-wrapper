REMARKS

Claims 1-9, 11-16 and 18-22 are pending. The present response is directed to
the Final Office Action mailed October 4, 2007. By way of the present response,
claims 1, 11, 14, 18 and 20-22 have been amended to further clarify the presently

claimed invention. No new matter has been added by way of the present amendment.
Claim Rejections - 35 U.S.C. § 103

In the Final Office Action mailed October 4, 2007, claims 1-9, 11-16 and 18-22
were rejected under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) as being unpatentable over Rasmussen in view
of Dartey et al., Lengerich et al., the book “PROFESSIONAL BAKING’ and Gage et al.
In response, Applicant has amended independent claim 1, 14 and 20-22 to further
clarify the presently claimed cookie units and dough products having a novel sugar
grind in which only about 18 to about 45% of the sugar (by weight) is capable of being
retained with a 100 mesh screen. Support for this amendment is found in the
specification as filed at Page 10, Line 29 ~ Page 11, Line 10, wherein a sifting
technique for achieving the desired sugar particle size distribution is described.

Within the Final Office Action, the position is taken that Gage et al. and the
Professional Baking reference demonstrate that sugar particle size affects the
properties of dough/cookies and that not only is the claimed particle distribution
merely an optimization within one of skill in that art, but furthermore, itis
conventional as disclosed by Gage et al. In view of the present clarifying amendments
to independent clairns 1, 14 and 20-22, Applicant respectfully asserts that not only
are the present claims directed to nonconventional particle size distributions but that
neither Gage et al. nor Professional Baking disclose anything supporting the
contention that the claimed distribution is merely optimization.

Within Table A of Gage et al. (Spanning Columns 7 and 8), nine conventional
sugar blends are disclosed, wherein not a single one of these blends satisfies the
presently amended claims. As described in prior responses, every one of these
“conventional” grinds satisfies the definition of being too course or too fine for use in
the present claimed cookies/dough. The Baker’s Special grind referenced in the office
action is well outside the presently claimed range in that at least 59.1% of particles

Purthenmore, neither Gage et al. nor Professional Baking provide any
instruction or suggestion to one of ordinary skill in the art as to how to approach
optimization of a dough unit/cookie unit that not only possesses suitable expansion
characteristics but also must possess suitable extrusion properties. For example, on
Page 248 of Professional Baking, expansion characteristics of coarse versus fine sugar
are discussed but there is no discussion or teaching of the effects of sugar sizing on
extruded dough let alone what sizes, distributions, etc. constitute coarse versus fine
sugar. With reference to Gage et al, there is similarly no discussion of suitable sugars
for use in an extrudable dough let alone the benefits provided by the presently claimed
sugar size distribution. Contrary to optimization, the development of the
cookie/dough units as presently claimed within independent claims 1, 14 and 20-22
(let alone the further limitations of dependent claims 11 and 18) required extensive ,
experimentation, said experimentation certainly not being routine in light of the cited
references.

respectfully request reconsideration and withdrawal of the present rejections and
allowance of the pending claims as it is Applicants belief that the rejections have been
overcome. The Examiner is encouraged to contact the undersigned if it will assist in

advancing prosecution of the pending claims.
