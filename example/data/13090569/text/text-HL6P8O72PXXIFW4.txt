## Claim Rejections - 35 USC § 103
The following is a quotation of pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(a) which forms the basis for all obviousness rejections set forth in this Office action:
(a) A patent may not be obtained though the invention is not identically disclosed or described as set forth in section 102 of this title, if the differences between the subject matter sought to be patented and the prior art are such that the subject matter as a whole would have been obvious at the time the invention was made to a person having ordinary skill in the art to which said subject matter pertains. Patentability shall not be negatived by the manner in which the invention was made.
Claims 1 and 3-5 are rejected under pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over Segan, US publication 2007/0256337 (Segan) in view of Lin, US patent 5,135,426 (Lin).
Segan teaches an interactive greeting card comprising a greeting card body having a plurality of greeting card panels 12, 23, 24 wherein two panels, 23, 24 form an enclosed cavity [0027] housing electronics etc. [0039]. Increasing the speed of the continuous motion of crank handle 70 allows a user to control the speed of audio playback.
Segan does not teach a back and forth motion for playback however this is well known in the art as shown by Lin. Lin teaches playback of an audio file including back and forth motion (playing the guitar) to control the tempo of the sound (column 2 lines 13-14). It would have been obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the art to utilize such a guitar playing motion on the card of Segan to provide a more attractive, fun card for the recipient.
Regarding claims 3 and 4, Segan does not specifically teach the type of messages displayed by the indicia or played from the memory however the particular indicia are considered to be entirely a matter of design choice. The examiner takes official notice of many types of greeting cards that include both sung and spoken messages as well as children’s toys and books which provide instructions. It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill to utilize the particular message desired to be conveyed to the recipient.
Regarding claim 5, Segan shows the greeting card must be in the open position to activate playback using the crank (Figure 3).
Claim 2 is rejected under pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over Segan and Lin as applied to claim 1 above, and further in view of Avery, US patent 7,356,950 (Avery).
Segan does not teach a slide switch to operate playback however this is well known in the art as shown by Avery. Avery teaches slide switch 34 which activates playback when 21 and 22 are opened. It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill to utilize such a switch on the card of Segan to provide an additional message for the recipient.
Claim 6 is rejected under pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(a) as being unpatentable over Segan and Lin as applied to claim 1 above, and further in view of Feuer, US patent 4,200,222 (Feuer).
Segan does not teach a card having an aperture in one panel however this is well known in the art as shown by Feuer. Feuer teaches a card including aperture 24 in one panel for viewing an inside surface of the card (Figure 1). It would have been obvious to one of ordinary skill to utilize such an additional feature in the card of Segan to provide further entertainment and decoration for the viewer.
