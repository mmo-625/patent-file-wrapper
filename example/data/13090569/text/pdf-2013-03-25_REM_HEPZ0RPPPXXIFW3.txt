112, second paragraph rejection of claims 1 through 7.

Il. The 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) Rejections:
Claims 1 through 5, 8 through 15 and 17 through 20 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. § 103(a) as

being unpatentable over U.S. Patent No. 7,356,950 (Avery) in view of U.S. Patent No. 6,084,569
(Ricotta). A combination of the Avery and Ricotta references do not result in the user-interactive
greeting card of the present invention. The Ricotta reference is non-analogous prior art. It is
directed to an editing interface apparatus. It merely disclosing a touch section of the apparatus .
wherein a user can touch to simulate a mouse-click to move between particular time intervals in
a video sequence. A continuous rubbing or back and forth motion is not disclosed by Avery or
Ricotta. Ricotta discloses a touch strip which allows a user to touch and also to drag his/her .-
finger across the touch strip to move between frames of a video. The video editing apparatus
does not playback audio according to the user’s continuous touch and does not cease audio when
the user removes his/her touch from the touch sensitive area. Claim 1 has been amended to more
clearly describe the greeting card of the present invention as having a touch sensitive switch
which requires constant user interaction to replay the entirety of digital audio file. This
requirement is not disclosed by Avery or Ricotta or a combination thereof. Claim 8 has also
been amended to require a touch sensitive switch which initiates playback of one of two or more
digital audio files when a human applies a continuous back and forth motion to an area of the
greeting card proximate to the touch sensor switch plate and ceases playback of one of the two or
more digital audio files when the continuous back and forth motion stops. The back and forth
motion required to replay audio is not described by Avery or Ricotta. Again, Ricotta merely
discloses a touch strip wherein a user can tap or drag his/her finger across for movement between
various frames of a video. Continuous motion is not required for any audio playback and
removal of such continuous motion does not cease the audio playback. Claim 15 has also been
amended to require a touch sensor switch located beneath one of the at least three panels of the
greeting card, the touch sensor switch operative to control playback of the at least one digital
audio file, wherein playback of the at least one audio file occurs only while a user

continuously rubs an area of the greeting card above the touch sensor switch. As argued above
with respect to claims 1 and 8, the combination of Avery and Ricotta does not require continuous
contact with an area of a greeting card to cause audio playback. Audio playback is not controlled

by the touch strip described by Ricotta and Avery does not disclose any touch sensitive areas.
